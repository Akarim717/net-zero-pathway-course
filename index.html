The challenge
“We want to become net zero. What exactly are our emissions today—and what should we do first?”

This course guides you through answering that question with evidence from your own organisation. The goal is not perfect carbon modelling. It is a credible first pathway that can be discussed, improved and acted upon.

A footprint needs an edge

Before calculating anything, decide which sites, operations and emission sources belong inside the assessment. A boundary prevents double counting, unexplained omissions and a baseline that nobody can interpret.

Learn one idea

Three scopes, kept practical

Scope 1

Direct emissions

Fuel burned in owned vehicles or equipment, and refrigerant leaks from equipment you control.

Ask: do we directly operate or control it?
Scope 2

Purchased energy

Electricity, heat, steam or cooling bought for the organisation's use.

Ask: do we purchase this energy?
Scope 3

Value-chain emissions

Selected relevant sources such as business travel, waste, commuting and purchased goods.

Ask: is it material and can we estimate it?
Worked example · Greenfield Training Centre

Greenfield draws a useful first boundary

Greenfield operates one leased training building with 40 staff. It includes purchased electricity, petrol used by two company cars, refrigerant losses, business flights and general waste. It records commuting and purchased goods as future improvements because current data is weak.

Week 2 · 5 hours

Build your carbon baseline

Where are our emissions coming from?

2.1
The central relationship

Activity data × Emission factor = Estimated emissions

Activity data describes what the organisation did—kilowatt-hours consumed, litres burned or kilometres travelled. An emission factor converts that activity into kilograms of carbon dioxide equivalent (kgCO₂e).

85,000 kWh×0.55 kgCO₂e/kWh=46,750 kgCO₂e
Use suitable official factors.

The example factors below are illustrative learning values, not reporting factors. Replace them with current factors approved for your country, year and reporting purpose.

Worked example · Greenfield

Follow one row before building your own

What should we tackle first?

3.1
From accounting to decisions

Start with the hotspot, then test the action

A high-carbon action is not automatically the best first move. A useful priority balances likely impact with cost, feasibility, timing, constraints and co-benefits.
